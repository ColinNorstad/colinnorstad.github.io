Case study

Production-floor systems at a solar pilot line

At a perovskite tandem solar maker, I built an inventory kiosk tied to the company's ERP, ran a 20-node environmental monitoring network, and installed the gateways that link line equipment to the factory's data system.

Role
Quality Analytics Engineering Intern
Timeline
June to August 2026
Where
Tandem PV, Fremont, CA
Tools
Odoo ERP, PostgreSQL, Fusion 360, Slack

Tandem PV makes perovskite tandem solar panels. From June to August 2026 I was a Quality Analytics Engineering Intern on its pilot production line in Fremont, CA. I worked on four systems, plus an invention disclosure.

1. Inventory kiosk (Odoo ERP)

  • Problem: the pilot line needed shop-floor staff to see live stock and check supplies out in a way the company's Odoo ERP could track.
  • What I built: a barcode scan-to-checkout kiosk integrated with Odoo. It automates stock movements and sends low-stock alerts. It also messages employees on Slack when their order is ready for pickup, and notifies the inventory manager when a supply drops below its threshold.
  • Who used it: shop-floor staff, the inventory manager, and engineers across the floor. The brief was unclear and my first point of contact wasn't available, so I set up meetings with the people who would use it (my manager, the inventory manager, and engineers), researched best practices for internal inventory systems, and designed around their input. I worked with technicians to dial in the barcode scanners.
  • Result: live on the pilot line by the end of the internship. I also proposed a follow-on project to forecast which supplies will run low and which aren't needed.

2. Environmental monitoring network

  • Problem: process quality and yield investigations needed environmental data from across the pilot line.
  • What I built: I deployed and operated a 20-node network that streamed 28M+ readings into PostgreSQL.
  • Experiment: I ran a controlled experiment showing the vendor's air-quality index saturated during real solvent events. The raw sensor signal was then added to the production data stream.
  • Who used it: the quality team. I wrote the SOPs, and the team adopted them as its reference.
  • Pressure sensors: when my manager handed me pressure sensors to add, I worked with the CEO to decide where they would be most useful. Then I designed the enclosures, ordered the parts, and got them writing to the database.

3. CAD and fabrication

  • I designed and 3D-printed 10+ parts in Fusion 360, including a louvred outdoor radiation shield and a vented mobile enclosure. They extended sensor coverage across the pilot line, 3 labs, and outdoor plant areas.
  • The shop had limited 3D printers, so I created a shared print schedule for everyone using them and redesigned my parts to use less printer time.
  • I machined 15 monitor stands from spare hardware so technicians could track panels in real time on production-line displays. The technicians decided exactly where the stands went.

4. MES gateways

  • I documented the installation of MES gateways that link line equipment to the factory's data system. Then I installed, configured, and maintained them across the line, troubleshooting connectivity to keep data flowing.

Invention disclosure

I authored an invention disclosure on extending PV module lifetime. The VP of Quality sponsored it, and it advanced to a second revision for patent review. It's under review, so I can't describe it here.

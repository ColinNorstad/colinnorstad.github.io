Case study

Automated gantry for indoor-positioning research

Designed and built a remote-controlled gantry that sweeps a photodetector across a precise grid, replacing hours of manual placement and feeding the machine-learning models behind the lab's indoor-positioning work.

Role
Designer & builder
Timeline
Oct 2023 – Present
Where
Boston College, Govindasamy Lab
Tools
CAD, Arduino, Raspberry Pi, Python, ML

The problem

The Govindasamy Lab studies indoor positioning: working out where something is inside a building from the light a photodetector sees. Training reliable models takes a lot of measurements at precisely known positions. Collecting them meant moving a photodetector to hundreds of coordinates by hand, which took hours and added human error to the data.

What I built

I designed and built a remote-controlled gantry from scratch that carries the photodetector across a defined grid and records a reading at each point.

  • Mechanical: designed the frame and moving carriage in CAD, then built and assembled it.
  • Electrical: soldered and wired the circuitry, using an Arduino and a Raspberry Pi to drive and coordinate the system.
  • Software: integrated open-source databases and APIs in Python to control the gantry and photodetector so a full grid sweep runs automatically.

Using the data

With consistent, automated collection, the lab could train machine-learning models on much larger and cleaner datasets than hand placement allowed, and use them to build statistically significant positioning models.

What I learned

Hardware and software are harder to connect than any class suggests. Building something physical that has to talk to code meant thinking across the whole stack.

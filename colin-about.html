About

Hi, I'm Colin.

East Bay native, Boston College engineer, trip leader, marathon runner, and someone who has been taking things apart since before I could explain why.

Why engineering

I was taking things apart before I could explain why.

Almost before I could walk I had a TV remote in my hands, and it didn't take long before I was pulling them apart. Not to break them, but to understand them. That urge to see inside a thing, work out what makes it tick, and imagine it working differently never left.

I chose Human-Centered Engineering at Boston College because it pairs a rigorous technical degree with something bigger: the Jesuit idea of becoming what the world needs. Engineering in service of people, not just systems. I'm adding Computer Science because the most interesting problems now sit between hardware and software.

That shows up in my work: an automated gantry feeding machine-learning models, a dashboard that turned scattered supply-chain data into one source of truth, and a water-filtration pump with Engineers Without Borders. See the projects →

What drives me

Solar energy is the problem of our generation.

The window for meaningful impact in energy is wide open: the technology is maturing, efficiency breakthroughs are still happening, and demand is accelerating. I took Solar Engineering at BC and came away more energized than ever. The mix of materials science, systems thinking, and global scale is where I want to spend my career, first as an engineer inside a strong team and eventually, I hope, as a founder.

The throughline from a disassembled remote to wanting to build a solar company is one instinct: find something broken, understand it completely, and build it better.
